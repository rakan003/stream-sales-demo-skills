# Research and presenter material

Read when the user requests company research, a Notion explanation, competitors, a deck, or a presenter guide.

## Track the requested package

Keep a compact project manifest in the project workspace. Include the client identity, chosen nonproduction environment with merchant app and admin origins recorded separately, independently verified API/checkout/portal origins, expected organization ID, and one row per requested deliverable with its real URL, status, latest verification and remaining work. Store identifiers and outcomes, never secrets.

For a full package, cover the marketing site, both billing journeys, hosted presenter guide, Notion brief, Canva edit link and requested public view link. Use explicit states such as in progress, published but unverified, verified, and blocked. A registered site URL is not live delivery; a copied Canva deck is not an adapted deck. Update the Notion Project links section and final handoff from the same manifest after verification. Do not add unrequested deliverables or change sharing permissions merely to fill the manifest.

## Company and commercial fit

Build a plain-language account of what the business does: who buys, who uses it, what each named product delivers, and how value/money moves. Use the client's official product pages, current portal, pricing/help material, and credible supporting sources. Do not write interchangeable company praise.

A useful product map has: product name, buyer/user, workflow, charging model if published, defensible consumption unit, evidence, and open questions. “Pricing not published” is an acceptable finding. Separate a plausible Stream billing proposal from the company's current commercial terms.

Competitors should be comparable by product and buyer. Separate direct alternatives from adjacent categories; cite what overlaps and what does not. A broad list of famous SaaS companies is not useful research. Do not invent market shares, adoption, integrations, or a reason the prospect must replace its current payment provider.

For a UBB proposal, name the payer and explain one concrete billable action, why it matters to this business, the unit, and the arithmetic: quantity × rate = charge. Then show where the same units appear in Stream, if connected. Distinguish money held or passed through from revenue charged for service, and label illustrative rates as proposed rather than published pricing.

## Notion

If requested, use the Notion research/document skill and live connector to create or update the intended page. Honor an existing destination. If no destination is supplied and a suitable authorized location is discoverable, use it; ask only if placement/access is materially ambiguous.

Start with **Project links immediately below the title**. Put the website and both deck links together at the top, plainly labeled **Website — public**, **Slides — public view**, and **Slides — internal edit** when those access levels are verified. Then include every other existing, authorized destination: each story, Billing/invoices, hosted presenter guide, the exact Stream meter record, and the client's official website. Label pending items clearly. Never invent deep links or create an unrequested resource merely to complete the list.

Follow with a short plain-English explanation of the business, the product map, competitors by actual overlap, and the concrete UBB example above. Write connected full sentences and a short opening sales summary. When the user wants a 3–4 minute read, aim for roughly 650–850 words in the main brief, including the two short walkthroughs, natural speaking lines and a few discovery questions. Label current **verified facts**, **unknowns** and **proposed demo assumptions**. Keep material limitations visible; move raw IDs, logs and long verification history into a collapsed appendix. If the connector cannot save a real toggle, use a clearly linked technical child page rather than leaving literal markup in the brief.

Include a compact **Local files / agent handoff** section with verified absolute paths to the source checkout, research/manifest, assets/outputs and installed skill. Include project/deployment identity and environment roles where needed, plus a short continuation note stating what is done, what remains and the next useful checks. Use only existing paths; explain that local paths require access to that computer and are not automatically cloud-accessible. Keep detailed operational fields collapsed. Never include API keys, passwords or temporary authenticated URLs.

After saving, read back the page, every saved link and local path, and the main status claims. Confirm destinations, access labels and current statuses agree with the actual resources and project manifest; update them after publication or verification. Preserve the concise structure and always return the real Notion link in the final handoff.

If the user authorizes mailbox research, read only the relevant sales conversations, including junk/trash when requested. Distill the lessons privately; do not copy customer correspondence, identities or mailbox links into public sites or decks. Outbound sales claims show positioning, not proof of capability or prospect demand. Reading mail does not authorize sending, drafting, forwarding or changing messages.

## Canva

If the user names a design, inspect that exact design and its pages before editing. Use the available Stream client-deck skill when applicable. Keep the approved visual system, narrative, numbering, and native media unless the user requests changes. Reconcile slide-specific facts and payer-flow visuals with the implemented site.

Adapting a previous demo for another client means replacing product terminology, logos, screenshots, URLs, flow labels, consumption units, and presenter claims—not only the title. Remove old-client references in speaker notes and hidden/off-canvas elements where the tool exposes them. Keep future billing models distinct from features demonstrated today.

Verify saved changes by reopening/reading the design, checking the changed pages and representative unchanged pages, page count/order, and any video embeds. Return the editable Canva link. This skill does not authorize emailing or sharing the deck with recipients.

The public view link is distinct from the internal edit link. When the user requests a public slide link, create or enable **view-only** public sharing through Canva's sharing UI or a supported tool and copy the actual link. Do not grant public edit access to produce it; keep editing restricted to the intended internal collaborators. Verify the public view link without a signed-in session where possible, and report any unverified access rather than treating a signed-in preview as proof. Never invent a public link by replacing `/edit` in the design URL. Put both saved links next to the website at the top of the Notion brief, labeled **Slides — public view** and **Slides — internal edit**. If public access is unavailable or not authorized, state that status without changing permissions.

## Presenter guide

Keep the guide short enough to use during a call. For each story include:

- Exact starting URL and account context.
- Three to six actions, with the expected screen after each.
- One or two natural lines to say aloud.
- The matching Stream customer/subscription/invoice record and what to compare.
- Reset/reopen instructions and the few material limitations.

Example structure, adapt to the actual client:

> “You already have a way to subscribe. Here, the customer can manage the plan, seats, add-ons, and invoices from their own billing page.”
>
> “This second view measures the service as it is used. These are the same units recorded against this customer's subscription in Stream.”

Avoid claims such as “everything is automated,” “one unified invoice,” or “supports hybrid billing” unless the implemented flow proves them. Keep hypothetical extensions—agent actions, additional products, tiered discounts—separate from the live walkthrough. State which activity is seeded and which records/payments are real staging actions. Do not put this explanatory scaffolding across the customer-facing UI.
