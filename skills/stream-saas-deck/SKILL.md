# Stream SaaS deck

Build a deck the presenter can use with a technically sophisticated SaaS buyer. It should look like Stream, speak in the prospect’s own product vocabulary and show the working demo where available.

## Read and choose the story

- Inspect the supplied Stream SaaS deck, current client website, pricing, product names and any authorized product portal. A client name and logo are not enough research.
- Identify what they sell, who buys, how the customer gets value, current pricing and payment arrangements, and where self-service ends. Map existing billing, payment and accounting systems before choosing the pitch.
- Pick the relevant commercial angle: a new self-serve route, more flexible pricing, less in-house billing work, recovery, or enterprise collections through bank transfer. A client with a handful of enterprise accounts may feel little reconciliation pain.
- When signup requires KYC/KYB or approval, show the handoff explicitly: the client’s operations team approves the account; Stream monetizes the approved customer. Payment success must not be presented as approval.
- Use published packages where available. Put proposed prices and unverified product assumptions in presenter notes; do not present them as current client facts.

## Copy the actual source

Inspect the exact source and its pages before changing anything. Duplicate the deck and name the copy `Stream × <Client> — SaaS`. Work in that copy and preserve the source’s page order, native typography, gray backgrounds, amber accents, editable elements and existing media unless the user asks for a redesign.

Use the supported Canva tools for copying, text/media operations and saved-state readback. Use the browser for precise visual checks and unsupported editor operations. Read [Canva editing notes](../stream-client-deck/references/canva-editing.md) for fragile native controls.

Identify slides by content, not a fixed page number. Build a change map covering client names, product names, logo assets, screenshots, domains, speaker notes and old-client references.

## Personalize the SaaS narrative

Adapt the source’s existing slides rather than forcing every prospect through every feature:

| Slide topic | What the tailored slide should show |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | Stream and the client’s actual identity, with one relevant commercial reason for the conversation. |
| Billing models | The client product and the pricing model that fits it. Distinguish package tiers, graduated bands and volume pricing. |
| Customer portal | The real billing tab, embedded portal, plan/seat/add-on changes, cards and invoices. Show only supported actions. |
| Payment methods | The methods their actual customer base needs. |
| Enterprise transfers | Existing enterprise customers paying by bank transfer, with attribution and reconciliation. |
| Reconciliation | What Stream records and what remains owned by the accounting system. |
| Recovery | A missed-payment journey and what stops follow-up after payment. |
| Expansion | Relevant markets and currencies; distinguish presentment from settlement. |
| Launch and support | The partnership deliverables and implementation ownership agreed for this account. |
| Assurance | Approved statements and evidence for hosting, security, compliance and SLA. |
| Close | The journey to prove, owners and a decision or follow-up date. |

## Match the demo website

When a site is also requested, use [stream-saas-demo](../stream-saas-demo/SKILL.md). Use its manifest as the source of truth for products, amounts, usage units, links and implemented behavior.

- Use authentic screenshots from the actual demo, prospect product and Stream screens. Do not substitute decorative generated UI for the billing flow.
- Keep company identity separate from individual product identity. A company with several products should not be relabeled as one narrow demo product.
- Keep recurring and UBB stories distinct. Do not imply that a recurring seat change proves metered billing, or that an on-demand usage invoice closes a native cycle.
- For a proposed feature beyond the live demo, say what it would do in presenter notes and keep its status clear.

For an embedded raster that must change, inspect the original asset and read [screenshot personalization](../stream-client-deck/references/image-personalization.md). Replace all repeated merchant identities, including dimmed wallet backgrounds. Preserve the original phone geometry and payment-provider marks. New screenshots are preferable when the working demo already supplies the right screen.

## Verify the saved deck

Check the copied deck for the old client’s name, domain, logos, product labels and notes. Inspect changed slides at presentation size for typography, crop, spacing, alignment and legibility. Check representative unchanged slides and page count/order.

Save the real Canva design, then re-read the saved contents and inspect the final pages. An editing transaction or uploaded asset is not a delivered deck. Return the direct edit link and, only when requested and verified, a separate public view-only link. Do not invent a view link by editing the URL.

Give the presenter a short commentary table: slide, what to say, and the client-specific question or transition. Put the website and deck links together at the top of the private handoff. Do not send the deck or contact the prospect unless the user explicitly asks.
